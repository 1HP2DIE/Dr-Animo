DMCA & Legal notice

Dr Animo — Android client · last updated 20 November 2026

In short Dr Animo hosts no video, no catalogue and no files. It is a player and a browser for pages that already exist on the public internet, chosen by the person using the app. We run no servers that store or stream media, we upload nothing, and we are not affiliated with any studio, distributor, streaming service or rights holder. If something is infringing, the material is on someone else's server — and we will still act on a valid notice by removing that source from the app.

1. What the app is, and what it is not

Dr Animo is free, open-source Android software distributed as an APK. Its source code is public at github.com/1HP2DIE/Dr-Animo under the MIT licence.

IsA video player (Media3/ExoPlayer), a catalogue view cached locally on the phone, and an HTTP client that opens pages a user can already open in a browser. Is notA host, mirror, tracker, index, CDN, streaming service, download site or proxy. No media file is stored on, or relayed through, any server we control. Is notConnected to, sponsored by, or operated with any site whose pages it can open. Endpoints are configuration, not content: a build with no endpoint configured simply shows an empty catalogue and refuses to resolve anything. Is notAn anonymity tool. It does not hide a user's IP from the site they open, and it does not bypass paywalls or DRM.

2. No hosted content, no transmission

  • The APK contains no video, no subtitle file, no artwork library and no catalogue database — it ships empty and stays empty until the person using it asks it to talk to a source.
  • Every playable stream is resolved at playback time from a third-party page the user's own device requests directly, over the user's own connection. Nothing passes through infrastructure we operate.
  • We do not log, collect, sell or share viewing activity. There is no analytics SDK, no advertising ID and no account system in the app.

4. How to file a DMCA notice

We respond to notices that comply with 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3). A complete notice must contain all of the following — an incomplete notice cannot be actioned:

  1. A physical or electronic signature of the copyright owner or a person authorised to act for them.
  2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to be infringed (or a representative list, if multiple works are covered by one notice).
  3. Identification of the material claimed to be infringing, with enough detail for us to locate it. For this app that means the exact URL or domain the app would request — not the name of the app, and not this website.
  4. Your name, address, telephone number and email address.
  5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
  6. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate, and — under penalty of perjury — that you are the owner or are authorised to act on the owner's behalf.

Send it to dmca@REPLACE-WITH-YOUR-ADDRESS with the subject line DMCA notice. We can also be reached through the repository: open a private report if email is not workable.

5. What we do with a valid notice

Within 5 business daysWe confirm receipt, verify that the notice meets § 512(c)(3), and ask for anything missing. RemovalThe named source is disabled: the endpoint is removed from the shipped configuration, the resolver refuses that host, and the build is reissued without it. Because endpoints are configuration rather than bundled content, a takedown does not require arguments about whether the app "contains" anything. DownloadsWhere a build is withdrawn, the APK is pulled from the download mirror and the release is marked accordingly. Open-source history is not rewritten, but no removed build is offered for download. Repeat sourcesSources that are the subject of repeated valid notices are not re-added. This is a standing rule, not a per-notice decision. LoggingWe keep notices and our responses for as long as needed to demonstrate compliance, and nothing else about the person who filed them beyond the contact details needed to reply.

6. What we cannot do

Because nothing is hosted here, we cannot delete a file that lives on someone else's server, and we cannot stop a third-party site from being reachable in a browser. What we can do is stop this app from requesting that source — which is exactly what the steps above do. If you also want material removed at the source, the notice has to go to the host of that material.

7. Counter-notice

If your material was disabled and you believe that was a mistake or misidentification, you may send a counter-notice under 17 U.S.C. § 512(g)(3) containing: your signature, identification of the material and where it appeared, your name, address and telephone number, a statement under penalty of perjury that you have a good-faith belief the material was removed by mistake or misidentification, and your consent to the jurisdiction of a federal district court for your address (or, outside the United States, any judicial district in which we may be found).

A valid counter-notice is passed to the original complainant. If they do not notify us of a court action within the statutory window, the source may be restored.

8. Misrepresentation

Knowingly making a material misrepresentation in a notice or counter-notice can create liability for damages under 17 U.S.C. § 512(f), including costs and attorney's fees. We keep a record of every notice and will point to it if a claim is later shown to be false.

9. Designated agent

To rely on the § 512 safe harbour, a service provider must register a designated agent with the U.S. Copyright Office. Dr Animo does not operate a hosting service, so it does not claim that safe harbour — but it does maintain a working, published channel for notices (section 4 above) and answers every complete one. 10. Abusive or bad-faith notices

Notices that target the app itself rather than a specific source, that demand removal of source code, or that are sent to silence criticism will be answered with the reasons for refusal. Bulk automated notices without the elements in section 4 are treated as incomplete.
